    About

  • I am interested in the history, culture, and languages of the Ancient Near East. This website is primarily for documenting research/other relevant projects I have worked on, as well as miscellaneous language-learning resources. My Bluesky profile can be found here. Website last updated 10th October 2026.

    Primary Interests

  • History of the Achaemenid Empire: all aspects, but specifically Persian Babylonia, Lycian monuments and epigraphy, religious history and acculturation, royal inscriptions, and ideology of kingship. I am especially interested in aspects of interaction between the royal/satrapal administration and local power centres such as temples, and the perspectives that can be offered from taking a comparative approach across different regions of the empire.
  • Related/adjacent areas of the Ancient Near East or Eastern Mediterranean, especially the Neo-Babylonian Empire in the context of the so-called "Long Sixth Century".
  • Study of ancient languages: primarily Akkadian, Old Persian, and Imperial Aramaic, with secondary interests in Hittite and Lycian.

    Research Projects

  • The origins of satrapal authority in Persian Babylonia c. 539–484 BC: towards a new conceptualisation of upward ideological influence in the Achaemenid Empire (2026). To be presented at the UCL ANE Seminar Series in February 2027.
  • Research project focused on late Neo-Babylonian history (2025–2026). Currently in peer review process; TBD.
  • Governors, Raids and Boats: The šakin māti, the Sealand and Uruk in the context of Neo-Babylonian to Persian continuity (April–July 2025). 25,000 word project on the roles of officials in Babylonia and the ideological basis of the regime change, mostly analysing administrative documents and aspects of the Nabonidus Chronicle.
  • Artaxerxes II and Anahita: A Discussion of Transformations (October 2024). A methodological evaluation comparing Old Persian, Middle Persian, Avestan and Greek attestations of Anahita to understand the nature of the goddess in different sources.
